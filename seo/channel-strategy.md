# Channel Strategy — read this before doing any of the profile work

Worked example practice areas: appellate litigation, antitrust, sovereign debt
litigation, AI and professional ethics, social media class actions.

## The blunt version

**Directory SEO is the wrong primary channel for most of your practice.**

Avvo and Justia work because consumers with a legal problem and no lawyer type
"car accident lawyer near me" or "how do I contest a will in Massachusetts" into
Google. That search behavior is real and high-volume for consumer practice
areas. It essentially does not exist for sovereign debt litigation. No finance
ministry, distressed-debt fund, or institutional creditor has ever selected
restructuring counsel from an Avvo listing.

Four of your five areas are institutionally sourced. Clients arrive through
referrals from trial counsel, in-house legal departments, and the ranking
publications those buyers actually read — Chambers USA, The Legal 500, Global
Competition Review, and Benchmark Litigation are repeatedly named as the
referral mechanisms for antitrust and sovereign work.

If you spend 90 days writing Avvo Legal Answers for an antitrust and sovereign
debt practice, you will have spent 90 days well outside the channel where your
clients are.

## Channel fit, by practice area

| Practice area | Directory value | Where the clients actually come from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sovereign debt litigation | ~Zero | Chambers / Legal 500 rankings, institutional creditor and sovereign advisory networks, firm platform, conference circuit |
| Antitrust | Very low | Chambers, Legal 500, GCR, Benchmark Litigation; in-house counsel; referrals from trial teams |
| Appellate litigation | Low–moderate | Referrals from trial counsel who need appellate specialists; amicus work; bar appellate sections; your record of argued cases |
| Social media class actions | **Depends on side — see below** | Plaintiff side: claimant intake, including search. Defense side: institutional, same as antitrust |
| AI and professional ethics | **Moderate–high, and the best fit you have** | Scholarship (SSRN, law reviews), CLE/MCLE speaking, bar ethics committees, Law360 / Bloomberg Law commentary, and *being findable by name* |

### The one that needs your answer

**Social media class actions — plaintiff or defense?** These are opposite
businesses with opposite marketing. Plaintiff-side means claimant intake, where
search visibility genuinely converts and directory profiles earn their keep.
Defense-side means you are selected by corporate counsel and directories are
irrelevant. I have written the kit to cover both, but this single fact changes
roughly half the recommendations.

### The one worth real investment

**AI and professional ethics** is a genuinely emerging niche with thin
competition, live regulatory movement, and an audience that searches. The MA
Board of Bar Overseers has been publishing on AI hallucination sanctions, and
MCLE New England is running AI ethics programming. Attorneys and small firms
looking for ethics guidance *do* search, and they search by topic.

This is also the area where scholarship outranks directories. An SSRN paper and
a Google Scholar author profile will do more for your name in search than a
complete Avvo profile ever will, and they feed Chambers and Super Lawyers
research simultaneously.

## So what should you actually do?

**1. Create the Avvo and Justia profiles anyway — but as a defensive, one-time
task, not a program.**

Both are free. The reason to do it is not lead generation; it is controlling
what appears when someone Googles your name. Opposing counsel, judges' clerks,
potential employers, and journalists all search your name, and a blank or
absent directory profile cedes that result to someone else. Budget one afternoon
total, fill them completely, and then stop. Do not commit to a Q&A cadence.

**2. Redirect the recurring effort to the channels that match your practice:**

- **SSRN + Google Scholar author profile** — highest ROI for the AI ethics work
- **Bar section involvement** — BBA and ABA antitrust, appellate, and ethics
  sections; committee work is what Chambers researchers and Super Lawyers
  researchers both scan
- **MCLE / CLE speaking**, especially on AI ethics where demand is current
- **Law360 / Bloomberg Law commentary** — quotable expertise compounds
- **Amicus briefs** — the appellate practice's equivalent of publishing
- **Chambers and Legal 500 submissions** — these run on annual cycles with
  hard deadlines and client-reference requirements; if you are at a firm, find
  out who handles submissions and get yourself into them
- **A personal site with correct structured data** (see `attorney-schema.jsonld`)

**3. Treat Super Lawyers as a downstream outcome.** Rising Stars (under 40 or
≤10 years practicing) is the realistic target. You cannot self-nominate. The
research department scans databases, online sources, and trade publications for
credentials — which is to say the work in item 2 *is* the Super Lawyers
strategy. There is no separate one.

## A note on how you describe the practice

Listing five sophisticated areas is fine if you genuinely work in all five. Two
cautions, neither of them about honesty:

**SEO dilution.** Avvo only surfaces you in categories you select, and spreading
across five areas means ranking in none. Pick two for the directories — the ones
with actual search demand — and let your site and scholarship carry the rest.

**Rule 7.1's omission clause.** The risk in a practice mix like yours is not
false statements; it is describing *matter types* without describing *your role*
in them. "Sovereign debt litigation" reads differently coming from lead counsel
than from a junior member of a team, and copy that is technically true but lets
a reader infer the former when the latter is accurate is exactly what the
omission clause reaches. Write your role, not just the matter.

Verify your admission year against your own BBO record before it goes in any
profile field. (mass.gov and massbbo.org were unreachable from the environment
this kit was written in, so nothing here is derived from them.)
