# Directory Profile Playbook (Massachusetts)

Ranking levers, platform mechanics, and a sequenced plan. Sources are listed at
the bottom. Claims I could not verify against primary sources are marked
`[verify]`.

> **Read `channel-strategy.md` first.** This file describes how to do directory
> SEO well. Whether you *should* invest heavily in it is a separate question,
> and for an appellate / antitrust / sovereign debt practice the answer is
> largely no. The mechanics below are correct; the effort allocation in section
> 6 has been set accordingly. Do not read the Q&A cadence advice in sections 1
> and 2 as a recommendation to adopt it — it is what a high-intensity directory
> program looks like, which is the right program for a consumer practice and
> the wrong one for yours.

---

## 0. What "SEO" actually means on these platforms

Directory profiles earn traffic three ways, in descending order of value:

1. **The profile page itself ranks in Google** for long-tail queries
   ("estate planning lawyer Somerville MA", "how do I make a will in
   Massachusetts"). Avvo and Justia both sit on high-authority domains, so a
   well-filled profile can outrank a small firm's own site for its own name and
   for niche queries.
2. **Internal directory search.** Someone browsing Avvo for "Wills lawyers in
   Boston" sees a ranked list. Completeness, reviews, endorsements, and activity
   drive that ordering.
3. **Citation and referral signals back to your own site.** Consistent
   Name/Address/Phone across directories is a documented local-SEO factor. The
   outbound links are mostly `nofollow`/`sponsored` on these platforms, so treat
   them as citation and referral assets, not as link equity. Some sources claim
   Justia passes dofollow links; reporting conflicts, so do not build a strategy
   on it. `[verify]`

Lever #1 is where the leverage is, and it is driven mostly by **published
content on the platform** (Q&A answers, legal guides), not by the bio.

## 1. Avvo

### Mechanics
- Profiles are auto-created from public bar records for most attorneys with at
  least a year of practice. Claiming is **free** and takes minutes.
- To claim: find your profile, click "Claim", verify.
- If no profile exists, you can create one as a licensed attorney.

### The Avvo Rating
Algorithmic, 1.0–10.0. It rewards **data you supply**, which is why unclaimed
profiles score badly. Inputs include years of experience, professional conduct
record, and self-reported industry recognition. Practically, every one of these
fields moves it:

- [ ] Years licensed + all jurisdictions of admission
- [ ] Every bar association and professional membership
- [ ] Awards and honors (with granting body and year)
- [ ] Publications — this repository's MA will generator is a legitimate entry
- [ ] Speaking engagements, CLE presentations, panels
- [ ] Pro bono and legal aid work
- [ ] Languages spoken
- [ ] Work experience, with no unexplained gaps

Fill **all** of it. A half-filled profile is the most common failure mode and
the cheapest one to fix.

### The highest-leverage lever: Legal Answers
Avvo's Q&A ("Legal Answers") is where the search traffic is. Individual answer
threads rank in Google for exactly the long-tail questions prospective clients
type. Sustained answering earns **Top Contributor** badges by practice area,
which also lift internal ranking.

Target for a consumer practice: 3–5 answers per week, narrowly scoped to
Massachusetts. **Not recommended for your practice** — see section 6 and
`channel-strategy.md`. Avvo's Q&A volume is in family, criminal, and injury law;
there is close to nothing to answer in antitrust or sovereign debt, and your
AI ethics audience is other lawyers, who are not on Avvo asking questions. Answer questions you can answer *better than the other three
responders* — MA-specific procedure, statutory cites, local court practice.
Generic answers do not rank and do not convert.

### Legal Guides
Long-form explainers hosted on avvo.com. This is the closest thing to a blog on
a domain far stronger than your own. Write the guides you would have written for
your own site, and cross-link.

### Reviews and endorsements
- **Client reviews**: request after matter conclusion. Check MA Rule 7.1 before
  soliciting or republishing any testimonial.
- **Peer endorsements**: attorney-to-attorney, uncapped, and the easiest
  reciprocal ask in the legal marketing world. Ten endorsements is a realistic
  first-quarter target.

### Practice areas
Avvo asks you to allocate practice area percentages summing to 100. Do not
spread across eight areas. Two or three concentrated areas rank; eight at 12.5%
each rank nowhere.

## 2. Justia

### Mechanics
- Free profile, no paid tier required. Claim by email, text, phone, or photo —
  email and text verify almost instantly.
- **If no profile exists, request one.** Justia will create a free listing;
  allow a few business days. This is the step most attorneys do not know about.
- Claimed-profile holders can join Justia Connect and participate in
  **Ask A Lawyer**.

### Fields that matter
Practice areas, jurisdictions admitted, education, experience, professional
associations, languages, fee arrangements, office locations, website, and social
profiles. Justia's profile template is more structured than Avvo's, which means
completeness maps more directly onto how the page renders and indexes.

### Ask A Lawyer
Same logic as Avvo Legal Answers: answer threads rank, and the answers accrue to
your profile. If you are budgeting time, Avvo Q&A generally has more volume;
Justia answers are less contested. Doing both is not twice the work — the same
research supports an answer on each.

### Note on Justia's paid tier
Justia sells premium directory placement. That is an advertising purchase, not
an SEO tactic. Evaluate it on cost-per-lead, and see the ethics note below.

## 3. Super Lawyers — read this before spending any time here

**You cannot create, buy, or optimize a Super Lawyers profile.**

The selection process is:
- Attorneys **cannot nominate themselves**. This is deliberate, to prevent
  gaming.
- Candidates enter the pool by peer nomination or by being identified by Super
  Lawyers' research staff.
- Candidates are evaluated on 12 indicators of peer recognition and professional
  achievement, then peer-evaluated by practice area, then blue-ribbon reviewed.
- **Rising Stars** is the sub-list for attorneys who are under 40 or have
  practiced 10 years or fewer — the realistic target for a newer attorney.
- In-firm nominations only count when matched by at least one out-of-firm
  nomination.

So the actionable work is not copy. It is:

1. **Be findable by the research department.** Their staff searches databases,
   online sources, and trade publications for honors and credentials. A strong
   Avvo/Justia footprint, published writing, bar-section involvement, and CLE
   speaking are literally the inputs they scan. Your directory work feeds this.
2. **Cultivate out-of-firm peers who would nominate you** — opposing counsel,
   co-counsel, bar section colleagues. Nominations open annually.
3. **Only if selected**: complete the auto-generated profile, and observe the
   badge-usage rules in the ethics section below.

Treat Super Lawyers as a 12–24 month outcome of doing the other work well, not
as a task.

## 4. Massachusetts ethics constraints on the copy

These are not optional, and they bind directory profiles exactly as they bind a
TV ad.

**Rule 7.1 — Communications concerning a lawyer's services.** No false or
misleading communication. A communication is misleading if it contains a
material misrepresentation of fact or law, *or omits a fact necessary to make
the statement as a whole not materially misleading*. The omission clause is what
catches aggressive profile copy — a true-but-incomplete claim still violates it.

**Rule 7.4 — Communication of fields of practice.** You may state the fields you
practice in. You may **not** hold yourself out as a *specialist* or *certified*
unless the statement is truthful, and if you claim certification you must name
the certifying organization and state that it is a private organization whose
standards are not regulated by the Commonwealth of Massachusetts (or identify it
as a named governmental body).

Practical consequences for the copy:

- Ban these words unless separately substantiated: *specialist*, *expert*,
  *certified*, *best*, *top*, *leading*, *#1*, *aggressive winner*, *guaranteed*.
- Any award or rating you display (Avvo Rating, Super Lawyers, Rising Stars)
  needs the granting organization named. If you display a Super Lawyers badge,
  pair it with the Rule 7.4 private-organization disclosure.
- Past results do not imply future results — say so if you list any.
- Client testimonials are the highest-risk element on the page. Review Rule 7.1
  before republishing any. `[verify]`

**Rule 7.2 — paying for recommendations.** Paid directory *advertising* is
generally permissible as the reasonable cost of advertisements; paying for
*referrals* or per-lead arrangements is treated differently and has a contested
history with legal directories specifically. Before you buy any premium
placement or pay-per-lead product from Avvo or Justia, read the current text of
MA Rule 7.2(b) and check for BBO guidance. I could not retrieve the official
rule text in this session (mass.gov is blocked by this environment's network
policy), so I am not going to paraphrase it for you. `[verify]`

Rule text: https://www.mass.gov/supreme-judicial-court-rules/rules-of-professional-conduct-rule-71-communications-concerning-a-lawyers-services

## 5. NAP consistency — the boring part that actually works

Local search treats your Name/Address/Phone as a fingerprint. Inconsistent
records ("Suite 4" vs "Ste. 4", two different phone numbers) split the signal.

Pick one canonical form, record it in `citation-tracker.csv`, and use it
*character for character* on every platform: Avvo, Justia, Google Business
Profile, Martindale, FindLaw, LinkedIn, the MA BBO record, your own site's
footer, and your JSON-LD.

This is worth more than any single piece of profile copy and takes an afternoon.

## 6. Ninety-day sequence — calibrated to this practice

This is **not** the high-intensity directory program described in sections 1–2.
That program suits a consumer practice. Yours is institutional, so the directory
work is a bounded one-time task and the recurring effort goes elsewhere. See
`channel-strategy.md` for the reasoning.

**Week 1 — Directory work, and then it is done**
- [ ] Verify your BBO registration address is current (it feeds auto-generation)
- [ ] Create the Avvo profile; fill every field; categories and weights per
      `practice-area-mapping.md` (Appeals 50 / Professional Ethics 30 /
      Antitrust 20)
- [ ] Request the free Justia profile; fill every field when it arrives
- [ ] Same professional headshot on both, plus LinkedIn
- [ ] Sovereign debt and social media class action work goes in the bio free
      text, not in category selections
- [ ] Run the compliance checklist in `profile-copy-templates.md`
- [ ] Record the canonical NAP in `citation-tracker.csv` and make everything match

Budget: one afternoon. Then stop. Do not commit to an answering cadence.

**Weeks 2–4 — Foundation for the channels that matter**
- [ ] Create SSRN author page and Google Scholar profile
- [ ] Deploy `attorney-schema.jsonld`; fill `sameAs` with the new Avvo, Justia,
      and LinkedIn URLs — this is what links your name across properties
- [ ] Join BBA and/or ABA sections: antitrust, appellate, ethics
- [ ] Find out who handles Chambers and Legal 500 submissions at your firm, and
      what their deadlines are

**Weeks 5–12 — Build in the AI ethics niche**
- [ ] Draft one substantial piece on AI and professional responsibility. This is
      the thin-competition, live-demand area where a written piece can actually
      rank and be read by someone who might hire you.
- [ ] Pitch an MCLE New England panel — they are running AI ethics programming
- [ ] Pitch a Law360 or Bloomberg Law byline or get on their source lists
- [ ] Volunteer for an amicus brief in your appellate wheelhouse
- [ ] Identify out-of-firm peers for the next Super Lawyers nomination cycle —
      opposing counsel, co-counsel, section colleagues

**Measurement.** Before starting, record: Google results for your exact name
(what ranks now, and what you would rather ranked), Search Console impressions
if you have a site, and whether you appear in Chambers/Legal 500 at all. At day
90 the honest success metric is not directory traffic — it is whether a person
who searches your name finds a coherent professional identity, and whether you
are in the pipeline for the rankings that your clients read. Without the
baseline you will not know, and "it feels busier" is not a result.

**What success does not look like:** Avvo profile views. For this practice they
are a vanity metric. Do not optimize toward them.
